👄 Aussie Vowel Sounds

Vowels are the biggest difference between accents. Here are some of the features people most often notice in Australian English. Listen to real speakers too, because accents vary by region and by person.

Sounds that stand outThe R soundRegional differences

Sounds that stand out

day, mate, g'dayThe vowel starts lower and more open than in many other accents. That is why "g'day" can sound a bit like "g'dye" to some listeners. Full phrase page →
no, go, goingThe vowel glides and often starts from a more central sound, closer to "uh" than a rounded "o".
too, new, blueMany Australians say these vowels further forward in the mouth, so they sound less rounded.

The R sound

car, water, teacherAustralian English is non-rhotic. The R is not pronounced at the end of a word or before a consonant.
water, betterA T between vowels is often pronounced quickly, so "water" can sound a bit like "WAW-duh".
far awayBefore a vowel, the R can appear to link the words together.
Tip: Don't copy sounds from a spelling. Listen first, repeat the sound slowly, then put it into a full sentence.

Regional differences

dance, chance, graphSome Australians, especially in South Australia, use a longer "ah" sound. Others, such as many in Sydney, use a shorter "a" sound.
Both are AustralianNeither is wrong. Choose one model and stay consistent while you practise.
